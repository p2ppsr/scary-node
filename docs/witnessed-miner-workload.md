# Measuring sustained witnessed-miner work

The design below preceded the experiments. Read the [mixed-age results](witnessed-workload-results.md), [interrupted endorsed run](endorsed-workload-interruption.md) and [cold-funding result](cold-workload-results.md) for what actually happened.

## Two comparisons with the same signed payments

The [production-settings replication](sustained-native-replication.md) supplied a baseline for four-node receipt and validation. Its 5,529,600 selected payments included 614,400 same-block spends, 4,915,200 spends of the immediately preceding block, and zero older confirmed parents. The [signed-input replay](evidence/chronicle-sustained-inventory-parent-ages.json) measured these counts. It raised a different question for a miner: can the entrant regularly issue nonempty work and settle its solutions under continuous native ingress? Two additional comparisons can separate the age of the initial funding from subsequent reuse. These are proposed experiments. Each needs a published registration covering its exact source order, software, setup, opportunity schedule, endpoints, acceptance and stopping rules before measurement.

| Proposed arm | Starting state | Wallet timing |
|---|---|---|
| Initially endorsed funding | All four nodes first select one fixed stock successor of the common h484 split funding. Publish its header and full bytes. | Preserve every signed input, the complete original offer order and its deadlines. |
| Mixed later reuse | Begin on the original h484 funding with zero successors, exactly as in the existing inventory trial. Preserve the first eight complete funding generations. | After 524,288 transfers, change only the order of complete source batches within specified groups, yielding both short- and long-lived outputs. |

For both comparisons use the same Linux release, four stock-peer-facing wallets, two separate stock Teranodes, stock SV settings and exact 5,529,600 transactions from the native inventory trial. Keep the 1,800-second offer interval, 225 Scary and 75 stock slots, independent wire readbacks, full source-membership audit, drain, two successors and resource sampler. The original cold, evenly spaced run supplies the common comparator. If testing a changed node binary, repeat the cold, evenly spaced condition with that binary to distinguish a code change from a funding or scheduling effect.

**Run the initially endorsed arm first.** It changes a single known eligibility condition while preserving all original ordinary transaction IDs and order. Scary can validate the first-level spends against witnessed outputs at the opening offer. Whether real peer delivery and subsequent reuse keep its templates populated is the measured outcome. A confirmed output with one observed successor is an eligible starting input under this policy. A validated, admissible pending parent may supply a child in the same template.

In the mixed-reuse arm, retain original batches 0–1023 in order. For each successive group of four complete generations (512 batches), divide the 65,536 rails into four equal quarters. Emit all four generations of the first quarter, then all four of the next quarter, retaining the 32 original 512-payment batches within each quarter-generation. Use this permutation for 19 complete groups and leave the final 48 batches in their original order. Each child still follows its own parent. At six batches per second the repeated interior gaps on a rail are approximately 5.33 and 69.33 seconds, replacing a uniform 21.33 seconds. The computed SHA-256 of internal transaction IDs in proposed offer order is `03848032e833aa5bf7ed51366454149f0ac00dfae349d380a701f60c32728391`. Use original source ordinals to compare identical payments across arms. The first 170.67 seconds retain the original cold-start behavior in this arm, including the first circulation of every rail.

For an ongoing miner demonstration, preregister at least one Scary-originated, nonempty, independently selected block in **each** five-minute opportunity interval. Retain the existing 3,000/s four-node adoption target and every missed, unready or rejected opportunity. For each selected solution publish the issued parent, incoming native transaction provenance, earliest valid witness context, body hash, payment count and later settlement. Report Scary-originated distinct payments per complete observation second, selected block share and each five-minute cohort separately. Meeting the mixed-miner target establishes regular origin and system-wide adoption at the measured load; a claim of 3,000 payments/s originated by Scary requires that rate in Scary's own measured numerator.

### Endorsed arm results

The eight selected Scary blocks in the observed prefix contained 84,210 payments. They all came in the first 50 work opportunities. Three later complete windows contained zero Scary blocks. The original enrollment was interrupted with 246 of its 300 opportunities recorded. [Measured outcomes, evidence limits and replay](endorsed-workload-interruption.md) The reordered arm completed all 300 opportunities and settled every signed payment. Scary produced selected blocks in all six windows. The system measured 2,974.15 confirmed payments/s. [Reordered workload and settlement readbacks](witnessed-workload-results.md)

## Separately measuring one large body

A 600-second arrival interval at 3,000 payments/s represents 1,800,000 ordinary transactions. Their exact signed bytes occupy 359,090,161 bytes. The built height-485 fixture uses a 130-byte coinbase paying a 67-byte locking script. Its 80-byte header and five-byte transaction count bring the full body to **359,090,376 bytes**. It spans 27 complete funding generations plus 30,528 subsequent payments. It requires 65,536 witnessed starting inputs and contains 1,734,464 same-block spends of earlier payments. This is a standard length-framed Bitcoin block below the extended-message threshold. Its burst validation, temporary storage and delivery demands differ from the 14.9 MB maximum in the sustained inventory trial. [Separate large-body registration and executable proof](live-cadence-block.md)

Prepare the signed body and Merkle commitment from file-backed bytes, offer one valid native block, and preserve independent byte readbacks and each node's timing, parser limits, script checks and explicit failures. An externally assembled body establishes receiving capacity. An entrant-originated variant also needs native admission for 1.8 million payments, a Stratum solution and publication of the same bytes. That second route requires its own basis for admitting the starting funding.

The original release's 256 MiB body cap and 2-million undo-item allowance are below this workload. A test-specific 512 MiB block cap, a 1 GiB journal and an 8-million-item undo allowance leave room for approximately 5.4 million witnessed creation, input removal and spend-marker changes. A separate origin test would also need capacity for at least 1.8 million pending transactions and around 359 MB of pending signed bytes, plus overhead for their indexes and immutable work. Validate the actual peak memory and checkpoint sizes. In the inventory trial's 14.9 MB workload the two Teranodes peaked at 10.15/9.52 GiB sampled container memory and the two Aerospike processes at 6.84/7.11 GiB. Those are measurements of the smaller bodies.

The workstation and Docker backing filesystem must retain their existing 20 GiB and 12 GiB reserves. Two fresh 9 GiB datafiles plus 6 GiB for retained source, checkpoints, bodies and measurement give planning figures of 44 GiB host and 36 GiB backing availability before provisioning. Observed physical allocation can differ from a datafile's declared capacity. At the end of the inventory trial the lowest measured availability was 33.64 GiB/45.29 GiB; recheck actual headroom before another enrollment. For a single 1.8-million-payment proof the earlier interval near 1.84 million additional payments used about 2.02 GB per namespace. A separate qualification can size independent namespaces for that one workload and explicitly report their limits. Preserve prior trial volumes and their evidence while qualifying a new configuration.
